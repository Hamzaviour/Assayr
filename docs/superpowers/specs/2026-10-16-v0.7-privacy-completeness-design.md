# v0.7 — Privacy completeness: DPIA, data subject requests, breaches, processors, PDPPL — design

Status: approved in conversation 2026-10-16. Builds on v0.6 (record of processing, PII inventory).

## Purpose

v0.6 records what personal data is processed. A privacy programme under Qatar's Personal Data Privacy
Protection Law (PDPPL, Law No. 13 of 2016) also needs: impact assessments for risky processing, handling
of individuals' requests within deadlines, a breach register with notification tracking, a register of
processors, and a view of compliance with the law's obligations. v0.7 adds these to the Data privacy module.

## Decisions (from the conversation)

- DPIA: **built-in template** with screening, sections, a risk list (likelihood × impact, measures,
  residual) and sign-off; a DPIA risk can be raised into the risk register.
- Data subject requests: **logged by staff** (no public form); due date 30 days by default.
- PDPPL: **a framework like NIA** — obligations with our own summaries, mapped to ISO controls and to the
  privacy records that evidence them; derived status; third framework on Compliance/Dashboard.

## Copyright and accuracy

The PDPPL text is not shipped. Obligations carry our own plain-language summaries and a topic reference
("Rights of individuals"), not quoted text. Article numbers are shown only as "Art." hints marked
"verify against the official text"; the README and the PDPPL page say the summaries are guidance, not legal
advice.

## Roles and permissions

New role `privacy_officer` (DPO) with `assets:read`, `controls:read`, `privacy:manage`, `privacy:sensitive`.
`isms_manager` gains `privacy:sensitive`. `privacy:sensitive` is required to read data subject requests and
breach records (they contain personal data); everyone with `assets:read` still sees counts in the summary.

## 1. DPIA

`Dpia` (one per processing activity, unique): `status` (`draft` | `in_review` | `approved`), `screening`
(JSON: answers to the 8 screening questions), `description`, `necessity` (necessity and proportionality),
`consultation`, `conclusion`, `approved_by_id`, `approved_at`, `review_due`, timestamps.

Screening questions (codes): `sensitive` sensitive data, `large_scale` large scale, `monitoring`
systematic monitoring, `new_tech` new technology, `children` children's data, `cross_border` transfer
outside Qatar, `automated` automated decisions with significant effect, `combining` combining datasets.
`dpia_recommended` = any two answered yes, or `sensitive`/`children`/`automated` alone.

`DpiaRisk`: `dpia_id`, `title`, `likelihood` (1–5), `impact` (1–5), `measures`, `owner_id`,
`residual_likelihood`, `residual_impact`, `risk_id` (nullable link to the risk register). Ratings use the
shared five-level words; score = likelihood × impact banded like the risk heatmap.

Endpoints: `GET/PUT /privacy/activities/{id}/dpia` (create on first PUT), `POST .../dpia/submit`,
`POST .../dpia/approve` (privacy:manage; needs ≥1 risk or a conclusion), `POST .../dpia/risks`,
`PATCH/DELETE .../dpia/risks/{rid}`, `POST .../dpia/risks/{rid}/raise` (creates a register risk titled
"Privacy: <title>", owner, links back; idempotent). Approved DPIA is read-only until reopened (new draft).

## 2. Data subject requests

`SubjectRequest` (per scope): `code` `DSR-001`, `type` (`access` | `correction` | `erasure` | `objection`
| `restriction` | `withdraw_consent` | `other`), `requester_name`, `requester_contact`, `channel`
(`email` | `phone` | `letter` | `in_person` | `other`), `received_on` (date), `due_on` (default
received + `privacy_dsr_days` setting, 30), `identity_verified` (bool), `identity_method`, `assignee_id`,
`activity_ids` (links), `steps` (JSON checklist `{text, done}`), `response`, `status` (`open` |
`in_progress` | `closed`), `outcome` (`fulfilled` | `partially` | `refused` | `withdrawn`, required to
close; `refused` needs `refusal_reason`), `closed_on`.

Overdue = not closed and `due_on < today`; "days left" shown. Endpoints `GET/POST /privacy/requests`,
`GET/PATCH /privacy/requests/{id}`, `POST /privacy/requests/{id}/close`. Requires `privacy:sensitive`.

## 3. Personal data breaches

`Breach` (per scope): `code` `PDB-001`, `title`, `discovered_at` (datetime), `occurred_at`, `description`,
`data_categories`, `people_affected` (int, nullable), `asset_ids`, `activity_ids`, `severity`
(`low` | `medium` | `high` | `critical`), `containment`, `regulator_notify` (`required` | `not_required`
| `undecided`), `regulator_notified_at`, `people_notify` (same values), `people_notified_at`,
`notify_deadline` = `discovered_at` + `privacy_breach_hours` (72), `lessons`, `status` (`open` |
`contained` | `closed`), `risk_id`.

Deadline countdown shown while a required notification is not yet made; overdue flag when past.
Endpoints `GET/POST /privacy/breaches`, `GET/PATCH /privacy/breaches/{id}`, `POST .../raise-risk`.
Requires `privacy:sensitive`.

## 4. Processors

`Processor` (per scope): `name`, `service`, `country`, `data_categories`, `dpa_signed` (bool),
`dpa_date`, `dpa_document_id` (nullable link to a Policies document), `subprocessors` (text),
`security_review_on`, `security_result` (`satisfactory` | `concerns` | `unsatisfactory` | null),
`risk_level` (1–5), `status` (`active` | `ended`). `ActivityProcessor` link table; activity detail lists
processors. Endpoints `GET/POST /privacy/processors`, `GET/PATCH /privacy/processors/{id}`,
`PUT /privacy/activities/{id}/processors`. Read with `assets:read`; write with `privacy:manage`.

## 5. Consent and retention on activities

Activity gains `consent_how` (text), `consent_withdrawal` (text) — shown when lawful basis is consent —
and `retention_review_due` (date) with overdue badge.

## 6. PDPPL framework

Data file `backend/src/assayr/catalogue/data/pdppl.json`: ~30 obligations grouped in 13 topics (principles,
lawful processing and consent, transparency, rights of individuals, security, breach notification,
sensitive data, children, cross-border transfers, direct marketing, processors, records and accountability,
privacy officer and governance), each `{ref: "PDPPL-01", topic, title, summary (own words), mapped_iso:
[...], evidence: [record kinds]}`. Seeded like NIA as framework `pdppl-2016`.

Status per obligation = derived from mapped ISO controls (like NIA) **and** the evidence records:
e.g. "Rights of individuals" needs the request process (≥1 request logged or a procedure document linked)
and no overdue requests; "Breach notification" needs no overdue notifications; "Records" needs ≥1 active
activity; "Processors" needs every active processor with a signed agreement; "DPIA" needs approved DPIAs for
activities where one is recommended. Evidence checks return `met` / `partial` / `missing` with reasons.

Endpoints `GET /privacy/pdppl` (obligations with status and reasons), dashboard/compliance gain the PDPPL
framework row. Controls page framework switch gains PDPPL (list only).

## 7. Privacy dashboard strip

Summary gains `requests_open`, `requests_overdue`, `breaches_open`, `notifications_overdue`, `dpia_due`
(recommended but not approved), `processors_without_dpa`.

## Screens

Data privacy page tabs: **Overview** (strip + PDPPL compliance bar + "needs attention" list),
**Processing activities**, **PII inventory**, **Requests**, **Breaches**, **Processors**, **PDPPL**.
Activity page gains DPIA section (screening → recommendation → editor → risks table with coloured
likelihood×impact cells → submit/approve), processors picker, consent/retention fields. Request and breach
detail pages with countdown badges and checklists.
